# Slice bible: one shift at Sallowquay Boat-yard

Draft for milestone M1, written against `pitches/pitch-a.md` and the sibling drafts `bible/world.md`,
`bible/cast.md` and `bible/systems.md`. Nothing here is canon, nothing here has been built, and no play has been run
against it; every beat below is a proposal for Rylee to approve, amend or delete at gate 4. No visual anchors are
chosen here and none are implied.

**Length target** is 30 to 60 minutes. The beat budgets below total 41 minutes, which is the floor of the target
rather than its middle: the slice should not outstay its welcome at any speed.

**Phase shape** is the tide's four hours in order. It opens in **The Turning**, roughly four in the morning, at full
water with the slipway under, and it ends at **Slack Water**, at the bench end of the causeway, with the town's
evening work lit on the far bank. Nothing skips an hour and nothing goes back.

**The one line the slice exists to test:** whether a build authored by subtraction - a shape made of other people's
cast-offs, with what was spent still readable in it - is legible and worth its loss to a player who has never been
told what the mechanic is, and who can afterwards say what they could not carry and why.

## Beat 1 - full water (Budget: 6 minutes)

**Place** -- The long bench under the lean-to, the rack of hooks beside it, the causeway head and the wall above it.
The slipway is under water and the wall is a cliff; the yard is wet stone and a pot being lit.

**Phase** -- The Turning.

**Who speaks** --
- *Tam Verrow*, who has just come across the causeway in the dark and says the first thing the kit-runner hears.
  He wants to be counted as a hand at the rack before Slack Water, and he asks for it by offering a mend nobody
  gave him: a coat hook he has already taken off the rack and already bent.
- *Warden Ottmar Vell*, who wants his bone fid back with the stem sound and not merely whole, so the packet boat
  can go across the channel on the evening tide. At this hour he states the ask as a fact about the boat and does
  not hand the fid over - the split in the stem cannot be seen at this light, and he is not a man who lends a thing
  he has not looked at.
- *Roald Fisk*, who wants the tar-coat mended so his sister can be brought across from the town at Slack Water
  without her feet going in the water. The blunt version of the ask, at the hour the world bible says asks are
  made before anybody has an opinion about you.
- *Grissel Toft*, at the pitch pot, who wants the pot down and covered with the collar bone whole before the water
  is over the wall, and who sets the pace of the hour by saying so.

**What the player mends or earns** -- Nothing that can be carried usefully yet. The kit-runner stands at the bench
with an apron, an empty satchel, four worn slots and exactly two verbs in the forms that fail: `Climb` in its slow
form, which announces him to anyone on the wall above, and `Wade` in the form that puts both hands under the water.
He tries to get down the wall and across the causeway and the two verbs fail in the open, at the hour when the yard
is emptiest and most likely to hear it. He refuses Tam's bent hook back into his hand, because it is not his mend
to make, and he earns the tar-coat, which is handed to him at the end of the beat and sits unmended in the satchel
until the bench.

**System exercised** -- Item-granted verbs, in their deliberately failing starting forms, so the first thing a player
meets is a capability that visibly does not do the job. The residents' phase-gated dialogue: this is the blunt
version of every ask the shift will make again later. The time rail and resting: the beat closes with the wait at the
bench that carries the hour from The Turning to Low Water.

## Beat 2 - the hour with light in it (Budget: 13 minutes)

**Place** -- The whole yard floor, which is walkable for the only time in the shift; the bench; the rack of hooks;
the foot of the slipway, where the packet boat is on the hard and can be worked on at no other hour.

**Phase** -- Low Water.

**Who speaks** --
- *Warden Ottmar Vell*, at his hour, with the shingle slate under his arm. He wants the fid mended sound and back in
  his hand before the evening tide, and he hands it over at this hour because this is the hour with light in it.
- *Roald Fisk*, at the bench before he goes under the hull, which is the only hour it can be done. His ask in its
  full form, as a story about who owns the coat and why it stopped working: his sister is on the far bank with a
  doctor, and the crossing at slack is the only one there will be this week.
- *Sable Kearn*, who is at the bench through every phase including the ones she ought not to be, and who wants the
  packet's hoist up once, in front of the town, at Slack Water. She will not say that. She says the gear wants doing,
  and hands over the piece that does not want doing at all.
- *Maud Bray*, eleven years on the bench, who wants her own scratch to still be on the thing she owns after the
  shift, and who says in advance and without being asked that fusion will take it off.

**What the player mends or earns** -- The two repairs, and then the decision. The fid's split stem, opened at the top
and closed at the bottom, mended so it is sound rather than whole; the coat's wax gone hard along both elbows and the
left shoulder seam open to the lining. Carrying the fid rewrites `Climb` from slow and audible to quiet, and takes
the slow deliberate climb away; carrying the coat rewrites `Wade` so his hands stay above the water and stay usable,
and takes the dive at the yard's foot away for the rest of the shift. That is three usable objects against four worn
slots, and the fourth slot is never filled: the cap is visible, not binding. The binding decision is the rack, which
fuses two mended pieces into one heirloom. In this slice there is exactly one reachable pair - the fid and the coat
- and before committing, the rack shows the preview and the preview is the only look the player gets: the heirloom's
  name as the bench would say it, its full verb list including the inherited one, and underneath, in plain text and
  named piece by named piece, exactly what is lost forever. The player commits or does not, and there is no third
  option and no way back.

**System exercised** -- Mending and fusion with its preview, which is the load-bearing interaction of the slice.
Item-granted verbs, at their first good form. The residents' phase-gated dialogue: this is the version of each ask
that arrives as a story about an owner, and the last version any of them will get before Slack Water.

## Beat 3 - the water coming back (Budget: 11 minutes)

**Place** -- The shed, the bench, the rack, and the causeway, which is now the only ground. The slipway goes under
within the first quarter of the phase and there is nothing at all across the water.

**Phase** -- The Run.

**Who speaks** --
- *Warden Ottmar Vell*, who wants his fid back for the evening tide. If the fid is in the satchel he is terse about
  it; if it went onto the rack as somebody's tool he does not ask for it twice, and his politeness about that is
  the punishment.
- *Roald Fisk*, on the wall, saying what the channel is doing before it has finished doing it. The ask at dusk, in
  the hour that is his: the crossing is at slack, the water is on the rise, and it is the coat's job and not anybody
  else's.
- *Margit Brack*, who comes across the causeway at the end of this phase with objects that have been carried in the
  cold for an hour, and who hands over the night crew's tin whistle. She wants one note that carries across the water
  to call the night crew in off the channel where the sound dies.
- *Sable Kearn*, only if the thing she wanted is what the rack took. She is present, and brief, and offers nothing
  further.

**What the player mends or earns** -- `Call`, which no other object in the yard grants and which takes nothing away
because there was nothing to take: put a name out over the channel and be answered back, without going near the edge.
And the crossing. The kit-runner goes out along the causeway toward the far bank and is refused - there is no
crossing at this hour and nobody on the far side will take a kit-runner off the water in a run - and the return is
the one that the shift's own work decides. With the fid and the coat together the crossing is quiet and short and
both hands are available. With one cheap verb kept on its own, it gets across slowly, audibly, twice, in front of the
whole yard, which is the same crossing at three times the length and with an audience.

**System exercised** -- Item-granted verbs under load, including a grant with no rewrite under it. The residents'
phase-gated dialogue: this is the last version of an ask before the shift is read out, and a resident whose hour it
was does not raise it again. The time rail and resting: the wait that carries the hour from The Run to Slack Water.

## Beat 4 - the crossing with a passenger (Budget: 6 minutes)

**Place** -- The causeway from the yard end to the town bank: the plank, the stepping, and the rest of it. Then back
to the yard end of the same causeway, which is where the shift is read out.

**Phase** -- Slack Water.

**Who speaks** --
- *Roald Fisk*, who is on the far side with his sister and cannot come across to help her, because the crossing goes
  one way this hour and he is on the wrong side of it. He wants her across with her feet out of the water.
- *Margit Brack*, who has the night crew to call in off the channel and one note to do it with, if the whistle is
  still in the satchel.
- Nobody else. The yard is quieter at this hour than at any other, and the people who come across now have come to
  collect.

**What the player mends or earns** -- Nothing new; the beat spends everything the shift made. The crossing out with a
passenger is possible in the way it is only possible at this hour, and it is possible in the way the build allows.
The quiet coat is what puts somebody under you and keeps the weather off both of you; the whistle is how a name is
put out over the water and answered, so the far side knows to wait; the fid on its own gets her across slowly and in
full view of anyone on the bank, one crossing at a time, and she is put down at the far end of it. The beat is
deliberately short, because the crossing is the whole argument of the slice and a long one would be an argument
about something else.

**System exercised** -- Item-granted verbs, spent rather than gained, which is the only way the slice can show that
what the shift produced is the thing that made the crossing possible. The residents' phase-gated dialogue at its
thinnest, with the people who have most to say standing furthest away.

## The ending beat - the shingle (Budget: 5 minutes)

**Place** -- The bench end of the causeway, with the town's evening work lit across the water and the slate in the
warden's arm.

**Phase** -- Slack Water.

**Who speaks** --
- *Warden Ottmar Vell*, who reads the satchel aloud and then signs it or defers it, on the strength of what is
  actually in it rather than on anything the kit-runner said. He does not fill a gap with a compliment.
- *Margit Brack* and the night crew, and the residents who came to collect what they lent. A resident whose object
  was fused is in the yard and hears their own name not read.
- *Maud Bray*, who is at the bench at every phase including this one, and who wants to know whether her scratch is on
  anything anybody is holding.

**What the player mends or earns** -- Nothing at all; this is the beat where the yard reads what the player did. The
objects are read in the order they were mended, each named for what it was for and what it did for the person who
owned it first, and each named for what the yard lost when it changed hands. An heirloom is read as one object with
two patches on it and both names still showing, because the yard knows what was spent and does not hide it. An ask
that was never brought to the bench is audible as an absence: the name is simply not read. Then the slate. **Signed**
if the satchel holds at least one heirloom - a build means a decision, and a kit-runner who mended and carried and
decided nothing is not read out for a signature; the name goes on the slate, the slate is held up, and the week of
nights is the consequence. **Deferred** if the satchel holds mended work and no heirloom: the reading is accurate, the
deferral is specific, the work was good and the shift decided nothing, so come back at The Turning. This is the
outcome the design is most comfortable with, because it is the one the slice is actually testing.

**System exercised** -- Mending and fusion, not as a mechanic being used but as a state being reported back in the
yard's own words. The residents' phase-gated dialogue: everyone on stage at this hour has most words, because this is
the hour they have been saving.

## What the slice deliberately leaves out

The far shore is a bank with evening work on it and nowhere else. No night shift, no roster beyond the week of
nights the signature buys. No second shift's worth of asks. No object that grants a number. The five items
`bible/systems.md` holds as backlog - the tarred line, the bend hook, the shuttered lamp, the bench knife and the
winch bar - are not reachable in this slice except where the open points below say otherwise, and no recipe is
authored for a pair the player cannot reach.

## Known limits this slice is built on

The slice is written against the current engine, and `research/A2-feasibility.md` is where these are recorded; they
are restated here rather than smoothed over, and none of them is fixed by this file.

- There is no wait or rest verb in the engine's closed action list, and the phase rail moves the hour for free, so
  the rail lock written in `bible/systems.md` is not enforced. The slice ships the four phases as **descriptive**: the
  asks are met at their own hour, and a kit-runner who is elsewhere when an hour passes does not get it back.
- Phase is in-memory only, so a reload rewinds the shift to The Turning. Nothing in the slice is balanced around the
  hour surviving a save.
- A rule cannot condition on the standing hour and cannot hide a resident, so nobody is ever asleep or absent. Who
  is awake is carried entirely by what each of them says.
- A line cannot react to a flag, an item or a place, so a resident's version of an ask changes with the hour and with
  nothing else. The reading at the ending beat is therefore reported by the warden's own line, not by the game
  explaining the satchel.
- The transition-payload trick that lets a resident speak on entering a phase works only in the woven player, fires on
  the transition rather than the standing state, and needs `"once": false` written on it deliberately.

## Open points for gate 4

Three places where the source drafts disagree with each other, or with themselves. This slice takes a position in
each so it can be read as a playable shape, and none of the positions is a resolution.

1. **Whose coat it is.** `pitches/pitch-a.md` and `bible/systems.md` name the waxed tar-coat Sable's;
   `bible/cast.md` hands it to Roald Fisk and makes his want - his sister across from the town at Slack Water
   without her feet in the water - checkable against that object and only that object. This slice follows
   `bible/cast.md`, because the crossing beat needs an owner whose want is the crossing, and it keeps the item's
   behaviour from `bible/systems.md` unchanged. The owner is unresolved.
2. **The bench knife.** `bible/systems.md` says `Unpick` is "the only source of `Unpick` in the slice" and that the
   knife is the price of every heirloom the player will build, then lists the knife among the five items that are not
   in the slice until gate 4 says so. The slice's centrepiece cannot be exercised without the knife reachable at the
   rack. This slice assumes it is in scope and needs the contradiction settled rather than assumed away.
3. **Which bank the shingle is signed on.** `bible/systems.md` puts the ending beat at the bench end of the causeway
   with the town lit across the water; `bible/world.md` says the town on the far bank is where the shingle is signed.
   This slice reads the slate on the yard side with the warden, because that is where the bench and the warden are.
