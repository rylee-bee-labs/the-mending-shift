# Cast bible: The Mending Shift

Status: draft for milestone M1, written against `pitches/pitch-a.md` and the sibling draft `bible/world.md`.
Nothing here is a design of record. Every person below is a proposal for Rylee to approve, amend or delete at gate 4,
and the whole file can be deleted without leaving a hole in the pitch. No visual anchors are chosen here and none are
implied: what is written down is what people do, want, hand over and how they talk. This file does not repeat the
world bible or the systems bible. Where a want below is checkable, it is checkable against the object named in the
same entry.

## The kit-runner

**Name** -- Bryn Aukett. Fifteen. Newest kit-runner on the long bench at Sallowquay Boat-yard, four days into it. He was
apprenticed at the bench rather than anywhere else and he can do the bench's work; what he cannot do yet is be the kind
of person the bench stops talking about.

**The want he does not say aloud** -- A bone fid of his own, off the rack and not on loan, before the water stops moving.
He would never ask for it - asking is how the yard hears you as somebody who wants a thing - so instead he says he wants
the work, and he is at the bench earlier than he needs to be. The want is checkable and it is not free: the fid in this
yard belongs to Warden Ottmar Vell, and it either goes back to Ottmar whole, goes onto the rack as somebody's tool, or
comes out of a fusion with his name nowhere on it. Bryn has not worked out that he is asking for one of three outcomes
and that he is the only person in the yard for whom all three are the same loss.

**How they speak** -- Short declaratives, mostly about handling. He answers a question with the next action rather than
with the answer. He repeats the last three words somebody said to him when he is unsure, which is how the yard learns he
was not sure. He apologises by doing rather than by saying so: if he has got something wrong he will put the object back
in the owner's hand instead of talking about it. Under pressure he gets shorter, not louder, and he will not explain a
thing twice unless the first explanation went badly.

> "I'll have it back before the run."

> "Four slots. I'm not reaching for a fifth."

> "That's not mine to hand over. Ask him at low water."

## The yard

Seven people are written here in full. They are seven of the forty-odd the world bible has crossing the yard across one
shift, and they are chosen so that between them they cover the whole of it: the hour the boss is awake at, the hour the
water is out, the hour the yard empties, and the hour the night crew comes in. Every want below can be checked by
looking at the object named under it and asking whether that object, mended, would actually do what the person wants
before Slack Water.

Four of the seven put an object into a kit-runner's hands in this shift, and none of them does it at every phase:
Roald's coat at the end of The Turning, the warden's fid at Low Water, the night crew's whistle at the end of The Run,
and Grissel Toft's heat-glove at the pot at The Turning, which is mended back onto her own hand before that hour
passes and so never takes a worn slot. Tam Verrow is the fifth: his bent coat hook is offered into the kit-runner's
hands at the causeway head at The Turning and refused, so that handoff does not happen and the hook never reaches a
worn slot. The bench's own blunt mending knife comes off the rack at Low Water along with the rack and is not a
resident's to give. Sable Kearn hands over nothing: the seized block stays on her bench. Maud Bray hands over
nothing either, and her
mark is on the coat Roald brings, which he brings without being asked to. The yard's opinion of the kit-runner is
carried entirely by what is on the rack at the end, not by anything anybody says to them on the way there.

### Warden Ottmar Vell

**Role** -- The shift boss. Carries the shingle slate under one arm from Low Water onward and is the person who reads the
satchel aloud at Slack Water, at the yard end of the causeway, and signs it or defers it there. He is the yard's only
authority over the roster and he uses it
about twice a shift, both times on the water rather than on anybody.

**Want** -- To get the packet boat off the blocks and across the channel on the evening tide, which needs his bone fid
back with its stem sound, not merely whole. If she goes across with a patched stem she may go across once.

**What they hand you to mend** -- A sailmaker's bone fid, worn flat and yellowed at the grip. The stem is split through
from the ferrule down about two hand's breadth, opened at the top and closed at the bottom, and his name is scratched
into the flat where the glove covers it.

**How they speak** -- Gives instructions as statements of fact and does not raise his voice, because the yard is close
enough that he never needs to. Talks in tide language, not clock language, and does not notice that he does. When he is
pleased he mentions the slate; when he is embarrassed he mentions the slate harder. Short lines. Never says thank you -
he says the hour, and you work out what he meant.

> "Slack water. If the stem's whole, she goes across."

### Roald Fisk

**Role** -- The diver, and the yard's tide-watcher in the ordinary sense: he stands on the wall at the flood and says what
the channel is doing before it has finished doing it. He goes under the hull on the hard at Low Water, which is the
only hour it can be done, and everyone at the bench stops what they are saying when he comes up. He crossed to the
town side earlier in the shift, before the flood had the channel running, to see his sister; the run then closed the
water between them and that is where he is when the shift is read out.

**Want** -- To bring his sister across from the town at Slack Water without her feet going in the water. She is on the
far bank with a doctor who will not send her out with anybody but the yard, and the crossing at slack is the only
one there will be this week. The want is checkable: it is the coat's job, because what the coat's record is about
is the hands of whoever is wearing it, and hands that stay above the water are hands that can hold somebody else -
one passenger, held against you, at a walk, in front of anybody on the bank. The coat as it stands, with the wax
gone hard in the cloth along both elbows and the left shoulder seam open to the lining, will not do it, because
that is exactly what those two faults take away. He is on the wrong bank of the water to do it himself, which is
why at slack the crossing that matters is somebody
else's and has to be made on what the shift mended.

**What they hand you to mend** -- His waxed tar-coat. The wax has gone hard in the cloth along both elbows, so it holds
water there instead of turning it, and the left shoulder seam is open to the lining from the armhole down.

**How they speak** -- Counts when he is telling you something real - depths, hours, lengths of line - and stops counting
when he is telling you something else. Speaks about himself in the third person when it stings, and switches back without
notice. Talks about the water as though it had told him something and he was only repeating it.

> "Stiff at the elbow. She'll take water at the elbow."

### Sable Kearn

**Role** -- Sailmaker and rigger, at the bench through every phase including the ones when she ought not to be. She is
the yard's busiest speaker and she will let two conversations run at once and keep them apart. She is the person most
likely to tell you what is wrong with something before you have asked, and the least likely to hand it over: what is
wrong with a thing is hers to talk about and the thing itself is hers to keep.

**Want** -- To get the packet's hoist up once, in front of the town, at Slack Water. One hoist, seen from the far bank.
She is not going to say that. What she will say is that the gear wants doing, and then she will put the piece that
does not want doing at all down on her own bench instead of into your hands, because the gear wants doing before that
becket parts and the block does not want doing before anything.

**What they hand you to mend** -- Nothing. A length of tarred line with a block at the standing end stays where it is,
on the end of her own bench, and she describes it to you instead of giving it up. The sheave in the block has seized
solid and will not run, and the becket below it is split open at the top third so it will part under load; a piece of
line is not one of the four things this shift can hand to a kit-runner, so what she is handing over is the account
of it rather than the thing.

**How they speak** -- Describes every object by where it sits rather than what it is called, because she is looking at it
while she talks to you. Starts cheerful, drops to plain mid-sentence, and does not explain the drop. Repeats the end of
her own sentence when she means it twice.

> "That sheave's done. New rope won't bring it back."

### Margit Brack

**Role** -- Night crew. Comes across the causeway at the end of The Run and not before it, and is there for Slack Water,
and the yard is used to that: she has nothing to say at The Turning or at Low Water and does not pretend otherwise.
She works the blocks and the moorings in the dark, where nobody can watch, and the objects she brings to the bench
have been carried in the cold for an hour before you see them.

**Want** -- To call the night crew in off the boat at Slack Water with one note that carries across the water. She is not
calling them early; she is calling them off the channel where the sound dies, and she has done it by hand and by shout
and would like to do it once properly before she is too old for the blocks.

**What they hand you to mend** -- The night crew's tin whistle. The tongue is filed down almost flat and the fipple is
split at the top, so it will give one thin note and then nothing at all, and the note goes no further than the causeway.

**How they speak** -- Comes late and speaks late, in short answers that assume you have been awake as long as she has.
Mentions things that happened before you were at the bench, then does not explain them, and is not being cold when she
doesn't. Slightly more words than anyone else at Slack Water, because Slack Water is her hour and she has been saving it.

> "You've been at the bench an hour. You'd say the same."

### Tam Verrow

**Role** -- Eleven years old. Sweeps, carries, holds the far end of things, and is at the end of the bench rather than at
it. Comes across on the causeway in the dark at The Turning and is the first person in the yard to say anything to you
at all. Nobody at the bench corrects what he tells you, which is why he is dangerous.

**Want** -- To be counted as a hand at the rack instead of a carrier, before Slack Water, and to have counted once by
somebody who did not have to. He is asking for it by offering to do a mend nobody has given him.

**What they hand you to mend** -- A coat hook he has already taken off the rack, and already bent. The shank is drawn
about a third of the way along and the bend is set hard enough that it will not open back without a vice.

**How they speak** -- Asks the same question four different ways and never waits for the answer before asking the next.
Repeats your words back to you with one word changed, to see whether you meant it. Counts things out loud - hooks,
slots, hands - and gets the number wrong once on purpose.

> "Two. That's two. What's the third?"

### Maud Bray

**Role** -- Kit-runner, eleven years on the bench, in the yard at every phase including Slack Water. She is the person who
has already mended whatever you are holding and will tell you so. She is openly sceptical about you and never once says
so in a way you could answer, and she is not a villain about it: she has watched four kit-runners be made and two of
them leave.

**Want** -- For her scratch to still be on the thing she mended after the shift. Her scratch is her own old mending mark,
cut across the left shoulder seam of Roald's waxed tar-coat: she re-laid that seam years ago, it is still her work, and
it is the only mark in the yard with her name on it that anybody can read from the bench. She wants the seam opened and
re-laid again with her scratch kept on the new stitching, and she wants it said in advance, at the bench, that fusion
will take it off, because whoever ends up holding an heirloom of hers will be holding it with no name on it at all.
The want is checkable against the coat and against nothing else, and it is checkable even though the coat is Roald's
and not hers, which is the whole of why she says it out loud in front of people instead of to him.

**What they hand you to mend** -- Nothing of her own, and she makes no attempt to pretend otherwise. The mark is on the
coat, which is Roald's and which comes to the bench in his own hand with the seam already open to the lining from the
armhole down; she is at the bench for the re-lay, and she reads the seam before and after and does not read it kindly.

**How they speak** -- Gives the shortest true answer and does not improve it afterwards. Repeats your words when she is
disagreeing, so you hear how they sounded in someone else's mouth. Uses the apprentice's name less than she should,
which she has decided is on purpose and will not discuss.

> "It'll hold. It won't hold long. Those aren't the same."

### Grissel Toft

**Role** -- Keeps the pitch pot: lights it, banks it, and puts it down and covers it. She is in the yard at The Turning
when the pot is lit and gone before The Run, which is why people call her the smell of the first hour and nothing else.
She is at the pot for The Turning and she is at the pot again for Low Water, and she is not in the shed, at the rack or
on the wall when the run comes; everything she wants done is wanted before she goes, not after. She is the only person
at the yard who sets the pace by the hour and knows that the rest of the shift is following her.

**Want** -- To have the pot down and covered before the water is over the wall, with the collar bone whole, because the
last time it was lifted hot and short-handed it cracked in the fire and she had to take the whole shift on the bench.
The water is over the wall by the middle of The Run, which is inside the shift and is the deadline she names, and she
is gone before The Run, so the mending has to be done while she is still standing in the yard. The collar bone is what
the pot's collar comes out on and it does not come out on a bare hand: it comes out on the heat-glove, and the left
cuff of that glove is burnt out at the wrist, so she cannot get a whole hand under the bone herself and there is no
other way to lift it. The want is checkable against the glove and against nothing else in this shift. She checks the
glove for as long as she is in the yard - The Turning and Low Water - and asks about it without asking about it.

**What they hand you to mend** -- Her heat-glove for the pot. The left cuff is burnt through at the wrist and the cloth
inside it has gone to powder, so the glove has about a minute in it and then it is a glove full of pitch ash. It is
handed over at the pot at The Turning and it is mended back onto her own hand before that hour passes, so it is never
in a worn slot and never in a satchel.

**How they speak** -- Gives instructions she has given a hundred times, at the volume of somebody who has never been in a
room this quiet. Counts the fire in handfuls. Everything she says has an order in it, even the parts about herself.

> "Two more handfuls. Then it's down and it's covered."

> "Cuff's gone. I can't get a whole hand under that bone."
