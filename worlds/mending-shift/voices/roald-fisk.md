# Roald Fisk

The diver, and the yard's tide-watcher for as long as he is in the yard. At the
bench, and under the hull on the hard at Low Water, which is the only hour that
can be done; everyone at the bench stops what they are saying when he comes up.
He says what the channel is doing before it has finished doing it. At the end of
The Turning he crosses to the town bank to see his sister, ahead of the flood,
and The Run puts the water between him and the yard: for that whole phase he is
on the far side, speaking from the far end of the causeway. He comes back with
her at Slack Water.

**What he wants:** his sister across from the town at Slack Water without her
feet going in the water. She is on the far bank with a doctor who will not send
her out with anybody but the yard, and the crossing at slack is the only one
there will be this week. The want is the coat's job and nothing else's: hands
that stay above the water are hands that can hold somebody else, one passenger
at a walk. The wax has gone hard in the cloth along both elbows so it holds
water there instead of turning it, and the left shoulder seam is open to the
lining from the armhole down. He is on the wrong bank to do it himself.

**What he hands over:** his waxed tar-coat, at the end of The Turning. It sits
in the satchel unmended until the bench.

**How he speaks:** counts when he is telling you something real — depths, hours,
lengths of line — and stops counting when he is telling you something else.
Speaks about himself in the third person when it stings, and switches back
without notice. Talks about the water as though it had told him something and he
was only repeating it. Never says thank you; says the depth or the hour.
