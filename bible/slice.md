# Slice bible: one shift at Sallowquay Boat-yard

Draft for milestone M1, written against `pitches/pitch-a.md` and the sibling drafts `bible/world.md`,
`bible/cast.md` and `bible/systems.md`. Nothing here is canon, nothing here has been built, and no play has been run
against it; every beat below is a proposal for Rylee to approve, amend or delete at gate 4. No visual anchors are
chosen here and none are implied.

**Length target** is 30 to 60 minutes. The beat budgets below total 41 minutes, which is the floor of the target
rather than its middle: the slice should not outstay its welcome at any speed.

**Phase shape** is the tide's four hours in order. It opens in **The Turning**, roughly four in the morning, at the
top of the small hours' water - the water up against the foot of the wall, the flood not yet over it - with the
slipway under, and it ends at **Slack Water**, at the bench end of the causeway, with the town's evening work lit
on the far bank. Nothing skips an hour and nothing goes back.

**The one line the slice exists to test:** whether a build authored by subtraction - a shape made of other people's
cast-offs, with what was spent still readable in it - is legible and worth its loss to a player who has never been
told what the mechanic is, and who can afterwards say what they could not carry and why.

## Beat 1 - the top of the night's water (Budget: 6 minutes)

**Place** -- The long bench under the lean-to, the rack of hooks beside it, the causeway head and the wall above it.
The slipway is under water and the wall is a cliff; the yard is wet stone and a pot being lit.

**Phase** -- The Turning.

**Who speaks** --
- *Tam Verrow*, who has just come across the causeway in the dark and says the first thing the kit-runner hears.
  He wants to be counted as a hand at the rack before Slack Water, and he asks for it by offering a mend nobody
  gave him: a coat hook he has already taken off the rack and already bent.
- *Warden Ottmar Vell*, who wants his bone fid back with the stem sound and not merely whole, so the packet boat
  can go across the channel on the evening tide. At this hour he states the ask as a fact about the boat and does
  not hand the fid over - the split in the stem cannot be seen at this light, and he is not a man who lends a thing
  he has not looked at.
- *Roald Fisk*, who wants the tar-coat mended so his sister can be brought across from the town at Slack Water
  without her feet going in the water. The blunt version of the ask, at the hour the world bible says asks are
  made before anybody has an opinion about you.
- *Grissel Toft*, at the pitch pot, who wants the pot down and covered with the collar bone whole before the water
  is over the wall, and who sets the pace of the hour by saying so. The water is over the wall by the middle of
  The Run, so the deadline she names falls inside the shift and not after it; she is gone before The Run, so the
  mending has to be done while she is still in the yard. The bone comes out of the fire on the heat-glove, which is
  what she puts in his hands: the left cuff is burnt out at the wrist, so nobody can get a whole hand under the bone
  until that glove is mended, and the glove goes straight back onto her hand at the end of the hour rather than into
  a worn slot.

**What the player mends or earns** -- Nothing that can be carried usefully yet, and one mend that goes straight back
onto the hand that brought it in: the heat-glove, mended at the bench inside this beat and returned before the hour
passes. The kit-runner stands at the bench
with an apron, an empty satchel, four worn slots and exactly two verbs in the forms that fail: `Climb` in its slow
form, which announces him to anyone on the wall above, and `Wade` in the form that puts both hands under the water.
He tries to get down the wall and across the causeway and the two verbs fail in the open, at the hour when the yard
is emptiest and most likely to hear it. He refuses Tam's bent hook back into his hand, because it is not his mend
to make, and he earns the tar-coat, which is handed to him at the end of the beat and sits unmended in the satchel
until the bench. One worn slot is filled by the end of the beat and three are empty.

**System exercised** -- Item-granted verbs, in their deliberately failing starting forms, so the first thing a player
meets is a capability that visibly does not do the job. Mending once, on something that goes straight back onto the
hand that brought it, so the first mend in the slice is one that never takes a worn slot. The residents' phase-gated
dialogue: this is the blunt
version of every ask the shift will make again later. The rest action and the locked rail: the beat closes with the
wait at the bench that carries the hour from The Turning to Low Water, and it pays for it in the two things
`bible/systems.md` says it pays for - the hour that does not come back, and the place at the bench.

## Beat 2 - the hour with light in it (Budget: 13 minutes)

**Place** -- The whole yard floor, which is walkable for the only time in the shift; the bench; the rack of hooks;
the foot of the slipway, where the hull lies on the hard and can be got under at no other hour, and the packet boat
is up on the blocks and can be worked on at no other hour.

**Phase** -- Low Water.

**Who speaks** --
- *Warden Ottmar Vell*, at his hour, with the shingle slate under his arm. He wants the fid mended sound and back in
  his hand before the evening tide, and he hands it over at this hour because this is the hour with light in it.
- *Roald Fisk*, at the bench before he goes under the hull, which is the only hour it can be done. His ask in its
  full form, as a story about who owns the coat and why it stopped working: he crossed to the town side earlier in
  the shift and the flood has since closed the water between them, his sister is over there with a doctor who will
  not send her out with anybody but the yard, and the crossing at slack is the only one there will be this week.
- *Sable Kearn*, who is at the bench through every phase including the ones she ought not to be, and who wants the
  packet's hoist up once, in front of the town, at Slack Water. She will not say that. She says the gear wants doing,
  names the seized block on the standing end of her own line, and leaves the block on her bench, because the gear
  wants doing before that becket parts and the piece that does not want doing stays where it is. She hands over no
  object this shift and the kit-runner gets none from her.
- *Maud Bray*, eleven years on the bench, who wants her own old mending mark - her scratch across the left shoulder
  seam of Roald's coat, a seam she re-laid years ago - to still be on the coat at the end of the shift. She says in
  advance and without being asked that fusion will take the mark off, and she says it about the coat and not about
  whatever the kit-runner builds out of it.

Grissel Toft is not in this beat. She is at the pot with the mended glove back on her hand, and the yard does not
need her at the bench, so she does not come.

**What the player mends or earns** -- The two repairs, and then the decision. The fid's split stem, opened at the top
and closed at the bottom, mended so that the stem is sound: closed from the ferrule up, whole and sound, which is the
condition the warden states and can check for himself rather than take on trust. The coat's wax gone hard along both
elbows and the left shoulder seam open to the lining, re-laid with Maud's scratch on the new stitching. Carrying the
fid rewrites `Climb` from slow and audible to quiet, and takes
the slow deliberate climb away; carrying the coat rewrites `Wade` so his hands stay above the water and stay usable,
and takes the dive at the yard's foot away for the rest of the shift. The bench's blunt mending knife is off the rack
and takes a worn slot of its own, because `Unpick` has exactly one source in this slice and the rack will not unpick
a mend without it. Counting the knife, that is four reachable objects against four worn slots, and the cap binds
on the path where nothing has been fused - at the rack by the end of The Run, once the whistle is in hand, and not
here: at this hour the satchel holds the fid, the coat and the knife, and the fourth slot stands empty until the
night crew comes across the causeway. Then a kit-runner at the rack holding the fid, the coat, the whistle and
the knife is carrying everything there is, and the next thing the yard offers has to stay on the rack. A
kit-runner who has fused ends the shift carrying one thing lighter, because the fusion spends two slots and gives
one back. The binding decision is the rack, which
fuses two mended pieces into one heirloom. In this slice there is exactly one reachable pair - the fid and the coat
- and before committing, the rack shows the preview, and that one look is the only look the player gets: the
heirloom's name as the bench will say it, `the quiet coat`, its full verb list including the two kept forms and the
inherited one, and underneath, in plain text and
named piece by named piece, exactly what is lost forever. There is no second look at the same loss afterwards. The
player commits or does not, and there is no third option and no way back.

**System exercised** -- Mending and fusion with its preview, which is the load-bearing interaction of the slice.
Item-granted verbs, at their first good form. The residents' phase-gated dialogue: this is the version of each ask
that arrives as a story about an owner, and the last version any of them will get before Slack Water.

## Beat 3 - the water coming back (Budget: 11 minutes)

**Place** -- The shed, the bench, the rack, and the causeway, which is now the only ground. The slipway goes under
within the first quarter of the phase, the flood tops the wall by the middle of it, and across the water the town
is still there and the crossing is still there and still passable. What is not there at this hour is anybody on the
far side who will take a kit-runner off it in a run, which is a different thing from the water being impassable.

**Phase** -- The Run.

**Who speaks** --
- *Warden Ottmar Vell*, who wants his fid back for the evening tide. He states it once more, in the flat way he states
  facts, and he does not raise it again after this hour: that is the whole of his line here, and what became of the
  fid is not said out loud by anybody, because the rack at the end of the shift is where the yard reads it.
- *Roald Fisk*, on the wall, saying what the channel is doing before it has finished doing it. The ask in the flood,
  in the hour that is his - the water is over the wall by the middle of the phase - and what he is saying is that the
  crossing is at slack, the water is on the rise, and it is the coat's job and not anybody else's.
- *Margit Brack*, who comes across the causeway at the end of this phase with objects that have been carried in the
  cold for an hour, and who hands over the night crew's tin whistle. She wants one note that carries across the water
  to call the night crew in off the channel where the sound dies. She is not in this yard at the start of the phase
  and she is not in the yard before the end of it; what she has to say is said after she has crossed, and again at
  Slack Water, which is her hour.
- *Sable Kearn*, who is present, and brief, and offers nothing further. Her hour was Low Water, the hoist has not
  been given to her, and she does not put the question a second time.

Grissel Toft is not in this beat. She is gone before The Run, which is before her own deadline falls.

**What the player mends or earns** -- `Call`, which no other object in the yard grants and which takes nothing away
because there was nothing to take: put a name out over the channel and be answered back, without going near the edge.
And the crossing. The kit-runner goes out along the causeway toward the far bank and is refused: the crossing is
there and it will carry him, and nobody on the far side will take a kit-runner off the water in a run, and the
refusal is given on the wall in front of the yard. What comes back is nothing - no passenger, no errand done
across - and only how the crossing is made is the shift's own work. That is not measured in this beat, where nothing
comes back to measure it with. It is measured at Slack Water, where somebody is put down on the yard end of the
causeway with her feet out of the water, and the shift finds out then what one crossing was worth - which is the
whole argument of the next beat.

**System exercised** -- Item-granted verbs under load, including a grant with no rewrite under it. The residents'
phase-gated dialogue: this is the last version of an ask before the shift is read out, and a resident whose hour it
was does not raise it again. The rest action and the locked rail: the wait that carries the hour from The Run to
Slack Water, and it spends the hour that does not come back and the place at the bench.

## Beat 4 - the crossing with a passenger (Budget: 6 minutes)

**Place** -- The causeway from the yard end to the town bank: the plank, the stepping, and the rest of it. Then back
to the yard end of the same causeway, which is where the shift is read out.

**Phase** -- Slack Water.

**Who speaks** --
- *Roald Fisk*, who is on the town bank with his sister and cannot do it himself. He crossed over earlier in the shift
  and the flood closed the water between them, and the doctor will not send her out with anybody but the yard, so he
  is at the town end of the causeway with nothing to hold her up on. He wants her across with her feet out of the water,
  and the only way it happens at this hour is if the kit-runner makes it.
- *Margit Brack*, who has the night crew to call in off the channel and one note to do it with.
- No one else on the crossing itself. These two are the voices at the two ends of the causeway for the length of the
  crossing and nothing speaks over the water but them; the yard is quieter at this hour than at any other, and the
  people coming across now have come to collect. The reading at the yard end is the next beat and everybody in the
  yard is standing in it.

**What the player mends or earns** -- Nothing new; the beat spends everything the shift made. The crossing with a
passenger is possible in the way it is only possible at this hour, and it is loaded the way the causeway is loaded:
out empty to the town bank, back with her, and put down on the yard end of the causeway, where the warden is
standing. It is the same crossing The Run refused, made on the strength of what the shift mended and with her
weight on top of it, so the build decides what one trip is worth to her. With the quiet coat fused it is
`Shelter`: somebody under you and the weather off both of you, in one trip, and nothing about the two of you
announced. Unfused, the coat's hands-kept `Wade` and the fid's quiet `Climb` are what she comes over on instead, and
she comes over one crossing at a time, with the whole bank watching every one of them, her feet out of the water
and her brother's face doing the rest. The whistle is how a name is put out over the water and answered, so the
far side knows to wait. Nothing in this
beat wants the line's `Haul`, the hook's `Hook` or the lamp's `Steady`: none of those three can be carried this
shift, and the crossing is built out of what can. The beat is
deliberately short, because the crossing is the whole argument of the slice and a long one would be an argument
about something else.

**System exercised** -- Item-granted verbs, spent rather than gained, which is the only way the slice can show that
what the shift produced is the thing that made the crossing possible. The residents' phase-gated dialogue at its
thinnest, with the people who have most to say standing furthest away.

## The ending beat - the shingle (Budget: 5 minutes)

**Place** -- The bench end of the causeway, with the town's evening work lit across the water and the slate in the
warden's arm.

**Phase** -- Slack Water, in the same phase as the crossing and straight after it, at the yard end.

**Who speaks** --
- *Warden Ottmar Vell*, who reads the satchel aloud and then signs it or defers it, on the strength of what is
  actually in it rather than on anything the kit-runner said. He does not fill a gap with a compliment.
- *Margit Brack* and the night crew, and the residents who came to collect what they lent. Everybody who is in the
  yard is standing in this beat: what a fusion cost is heard by all of them at once, because the yard reads it aloud
  and does not lower its voice for the name that is not read.
- *Maud Bray*, who is at the bench at every phase including this one, and who asks after the coat rather than after
  the build: whether her scratch is on it. She is not asking the yard whether anything is worth something. She is
  asking whether her work is still on the shoulder of a coat somebody is carrying.

**What the player mends or earns** -- Nothing at all; this is the beat where the yard reads what the player did. The
objects are read in the order they were mended, each named for what it was for and what it did for the person who
owned it first, and each named for what the yard lost when it changed hands. An heirloom is read as one object with
two patches on it and both names still showing, because the yard knows what was spent and does not hide it. An ask
that was never brought to the bench is audible as an absence: the name is simply not read. Then the slate. **Signed**
if the satchel holds at least one heirloom - a build means a decision, and a kit-runner who mended and carried and
decided nothing is not read out for a signature; the name goes on the slate, the slate is held up, and the week of
nights is the consequence. **Deferred** if the satchel holds mended work and no heirloom: the reading is accurate, the
deferral is specific, the work was good and the shift decided nothing, so come back at The Turning. This is the
outcome the design is most comfortable with, because it is the one the slice is actually testing. **Refused** if the
satchel is empty or holds an object whose owner came to the bench for it and was told no; the slate is not read for a
signature at all.

**System exercised** -- Mending and fusion, not as a mechanic being used but as a state being reported back in the
yard's own words. The residents' phase-gated dialogue: everyone on stage at this hour has most words, because this is
the hour they have been saving.

## What the slice deliberately leaves out

The far shore is a bank with evening work on it and nowhere else. No night shift, no roster beyond the week of
nights the signature buys. No second shift's worth of asks. No object that grants a number. Four items
`bible/systems.md` holds as backlog - the tarred line, the bend hook, the shuttered lamp and the winch bar -
are not reachable in this slice: none of `Haul`, `Hook` or `Steady` can be carried this shift, and no recipe is
exercised for a pair the player cannot reach. Not reachable is not the same as not present: the bent hook is off the
rack and in a child's hands at The Turning, and the tarred line is on the end of Sable Kearn's own bench at Low
Water, and where they are kept is not what this section is about. The fifth item that used to be on that list is the
bench's own blunt mending knife, and it is in the slice: it is at the rack from Low Water, it takes a worn slot, and
without it there
is no `Unpick` and no heirloom. The one reachable pair is still the fid and the coat.

## Known limits this slice is built on

The slice is written against the current engine, and `research/A2-feasibility.md` is where these are recorded; they
are restated here rather than smoothed over, and none of them is fixed by this file.

- The Rest action, the locked rail and the phase condition are engine work landing for this milestone, and
  `bible/systems.md` writes them up as `"rest": true, "rail": "locked", "persist": true` plus a rule condition on the
  standing phase. Today's engine has no rest verb in its closed action list and the rail writes the clicked phase
  straight into state, so the rail lock is not enforced yet and the slice pays the hour in prose and in the
  residents' lines: the asks are met at their own hour, and a kit-runner who is elsewhere when an hour passes does
  not get it back. What the slice is written as though all of this is true is that a phase move costs the hour it
  leaves and the kit-runner's place at the bench, and that nothing skips ahead and nothing goes back.
- Phase is in-memory only, so a reload rewinds the shift to The Turning, and the slice is not balanced around the
  hour surviving a save. `persist: true` is a request, not a description of what is built.
- The standalone rails fire no rules. A resident who only speaks at one hour does not speak at all if the slice is
  ever demonstrated on a served shell rather than woven, and the slice does not claim otherwise anywhere.
- A rule conditions on the standing phase once the phase condition lands, and on nothing else. No rule reads what the
  kit-runner is carrying or what the rack took, so every beat below is written as its plain default: a resident's
  version of an ask changes with the hour and with nothing else, and nothing anybody says here is a condition on the
  satchel. What that limit does not touch is what the items and recipes already say - how hard the Slack Water
  crossing is, and whether the satchel holds an heirloom when the warden reads it, are read out of the heirloom's
  verb list and the recipe records, not out of a rule's conditions on the satchel. A rule also cannot hide a
  resident, and a resident written off the stage is off the stage rather than switched off: Grissel Toft is not in
  the yard before The Run, Margit Brack is not in it until the end of that phase, and Roald Fisk is across the water
  for the crossing in Beat 4, and all three of those are said in the beats rather than enforced by the engine
  refusing to find them. Who is awake is carried entirely by what each of them says. The reading at the ending beat
  is therefore reported by the warden's own line, not by the game explaining the satchel.
- The transition-payload trick that lets a resident speak on entering a phase works only in the woven player, fires on
  the transition rather than the standing state, and needs `"once": false` written on it deliberately. It is what the
  slice ships on today, because the phase condition is engine work still landing.

## Three decisions this slice now stands on

Three places where the source drafts disagreed with each other or with themselves. All three are decided, and each
one commits this slice to something it can no longer be read any other way.

1. **The waxed tar-coat is Roald Fisk's.** `bible/cast.md` hands it to him and makes his want - his sister across from
   the town at Slack Water without her feet in the water - checkable against that object and only that object, and
   `bible/systems.md` now names the item `Roald Fisk's waxed tar-coat` to match. `pitches/pitch-a.md` still says
   "Sable's coat" and is out of this file's scope to change; the pitch is a mockup and the bibles are what the item
   data follows. **This commits the slice to** a crossing beat with an owner whose want is the crossing, and to the
   item's behaviour being unchanged from `bible/systems.md`: `Wade` rewritten to keep the hands above the water.
2. **The bench knife is in the slice.** It is reachable at the rack, it occupies one of the four worn slots, and it is
   required for any fusion, so `Unpick` is in the yard from Low Water and the slice's centrepiece can actually be
   exercised. **This commits the slice to** four reachable objects - the bone fid, the waxed tar-coat, the tin whistle
   and the bench knife - against four worn slots, with the cap binding rather than sitting idle on the path where
   nothing has been fused, and to the knife being spent like anything else if it goes into a fusion.
3. **The shingle is signed at the yard end of the causeway.** It is read and signed at the bench, on the yard side,
   with the warden and the slate and the town's evening work lit across the water, and `bible/world.md` now says so
   rather than putting the signature in the town. **This commits the slice to** nothing on the ending beat crossing the
   water to do anything: the far bank is where the work the signature is for happens, and the whole beat happens at
   the bench.
