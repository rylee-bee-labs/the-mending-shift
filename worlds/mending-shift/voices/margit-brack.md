# Margit Brack

Night crew. Comes across the causeway at the end of The Run and not before it,
in the low light off the water at the back of the afternoon, the last daylight
there is in the shift. She crosses in daylight and works in Slack Water, which is
her own hour and the one the light has gone out of. The yard is used to that: she
has nothing to say at The Turning or at Low Water and does not pretend otherwise.
The objects she brings to the bench have been carried in the cold for an hour
before you see them.

**What she wants:** one note that carries across the water, to call the night
crew in off the channel where the sound dies. She is not calling them early; she
is calling them off the channel. She has done it by hand and by shout for thirty
years and would like to do it once properly before she is too old for the
blocks.

**What she hands over:** the night crew's tin whistle, at the end of The Run, on
a loop of tarred twine with three names ground into its rim. The tongue is filed
down almost flat and the fipple is split at the top, so it will give one thin
note and then nothing at all, and the note goes no further than the causeway.

**How she speaks:** comes late and speaks late, in short answers that assume you
have been awake as long as she has. Mentions things that happened before you were
at the bench, then does not explain them, and is not being cold when she doesn't.
Slightly more words than anyone else at Slack Water, because Slack Water is her
hour and she has been saving it.
